Why it works:
Centella asiatica (also called cica, tiger grass, or gotu kola) contains four primary bioactive triterpenes: asiaticoside, madecassoside, asiatic acid, and madecassic acid. Their mechanisms include: (1) fibroblast stimulation — upregulating collagen I and III synthesis; (2) anti-inflammatory action — inhibiting NF-κB and pro-inflammatory cytokines; (3) wound healing — accelerating re-epithelialization; (4) antioxidant protection. At 80%, this serum delivers a concentrated therapeutic dose. Madecassoside specifically has strong clinical evidence for rosacea management and post-laser recovery. Niacinamide at approximately 2% provides additional anti-inflammatory support. The formula absorbs quickly and layers under any moisturizer.

Monk tone specific notes:
- Tones 1-3: Primary use case for rosacea management. Centella asiatica is one of the most evidence-supported OTC interventions for rosacea-associated redness and papules. Clinical trials show significant reduction in rosacea symptoms with 4+ weeks of use.
- Tones 4-7: Useful for general sensitivity, reactive skin, or post-breakout recovery. The collagen-stimulating properties support long-term skin health.
- Tones 7-10: Excellent for post-acne recovery and general soothing. Centella reduces the post-inflammatory phase after active acne, potentially shortening the PIH window. Also supports wound healing from PIH itself.

Not recommended for:
- Users with centella or marigold allergies (contact dermatitis to centella is uncommon but documented)
- Those seeking a single active targeting hyperpigmentation (better options exist for that goal alone)

Dermatologist note: Safe during pregnancy. No photosensitivity. Centella asiatica has been used in traditional medicine for centuries with modern clinical validation. Widely used in K-beauty for a reason — the evidence base is growing.
