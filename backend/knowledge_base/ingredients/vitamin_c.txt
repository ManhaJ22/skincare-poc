INGREDIENT: Vitamin C (L-ascorbic acid and derivatives: sodium ascorbyl phosphate, magnesium ascorbyl phosphate, ascorbyl glucoside, 3-O-ethyl ascorbic acid, tetrahexyldecyl ascorbate)

WHAT IT DOES AT A SKIN LEVEL:
- Antioxidant: neutralizes UV- and pollution-generated free radicals, which boosts sunscreen's protection when layered under it.
- Collagen: a required cofactor for the enzymes that stabilize collagen. Topical L-ascorbic acid stimulates collagen synthesis and softens fine lines over months.
- Pigmentation: inhibits tyrosinase (the enzyme that makes melanin), which fades dark spots and evens tone.

EFFECTIVE CONCENTRATION: L-ascorbic acid at 10-20%, formulated at pH below 3.5 to penetrate. Above 20% adds irritation, not benefit. Derivatives are gentler and more stable but less potent; they are typically used at 5-15% depending on the form. L-ascorbic acid oxidizes (turns yellow/orange/brown) with air, light, and heat. Discard it once it is dark.

DEEPER SKIN TONES (Monk 6-10): very useful for hyperpigmentation and PIH. However, low-pH L-ascorbic acid can sting and irritate, and irritation can cause new PIH on deeper skin. Sensitive deeper skin does better starting with a derivative or a 10% formula. Oxidized vitamin C can temporarily stain skin orange, which shows more on lighter skin.

PAIRS WELL WITH: sunscreen (morning layering is the classic use), vitamin E and ferulic acid (stabilize it and boost its effect), hyaluronic acid, niacinamide (modern formulas are fine together).
CONFLICTS: avoid layering low-pH L-ascorbic acid with AHA/BHA or retinol in the same routine. Use vitamin C in the morning and acids/retinoids at night. Benzoyl peroxide oxidizes vitamin C.

SENSITIZATION / OVERUSE RISKS: stinging, redness, and dryness with high-strength L-ascorbic acid, especially on sensitive or compromised skin. Occasional breakouts from heavy carrier formulas.
