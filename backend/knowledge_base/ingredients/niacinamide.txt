INGREDIENT: Niacinamide (nicotinamide, vitamin B3)

WHAT IT DOES AT A SKIN LEVEL:
- Barrier repair: increases synthesis of ceramides and other stratum corneum lipids and reduces transepidermal water loss.
- Pigmentation: lowers the transfer of melanosomes (pigment packets) from melanocytes to surrounding keratinocytes, which fades dark spots, PIH, and uneven tone without killing or suppressing melanocytes.
- Oil control: reduces sebum production, which visibly tightens the look of pores on oily skin.
- Anti-inflammatory: reduces redness and inflammatory acne lesions. It is a precursor to NAD+, which supports cellular energy and DNA repair after UV exposure.

EFFECTIVE CONCENTRATION: 2-5% for barrier and brightening (most clinical data is at 4-5%). Up to 10% for oil control. Higher is not proportionally better and raises irritation risk.

DEEPER SKIN TONES (Monk 6-10): one of the best first-line brighteners for deeper skin. It targets pigment transfer rather than melanin production, so it does not cause uneven lightening or a "halo" around spots. Its low irritation profile matters because irritation itself causes PIH on deeper tones.

PAIRS WELL WITH: hyaluronic acid, ceramides, retinol (niacinamide reduces retinoid irritation), salicylic acid, sunscreen, zinc, tranexamic acid.
CONFLICTS / CAUTION: the old "niacinamide + vitamin C cancel each other" claim comes from outdated high-heat studies and is not a practical concern with modern formulas. Some people flush when they apply both at once; separating them AM/PM avoids this.

SENSITIZATION / OVERUSE RISKS: 10%+ can cause redness, itching, or a temporary flush in sensitive users. Layering several niacinamide products can push the total concentration too high. It can pill under silicone-heavy products.
