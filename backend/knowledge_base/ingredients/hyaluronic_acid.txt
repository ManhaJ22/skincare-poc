INGREDIENT: Hyaluronic acid (sodium hyaluronate, hydrolyzed hyaluronic acid)

WHAT IT DOES AT A SKIN LEVEL: a glycosaminoglycan that occurs naturally in the dermis. Applied topically, it is a humectant: it binds water (it can hold many times its weight) in the upper layers of the stratum corneum. That temporarily plumps the surface, softens fine dehydration lines, and improves elasticity. High-molecular-weight HA stays on the surface and forms a hydrating film. Low-molecular-weight and hydrolyzed HA penetrate slightly deeper. Topical HA does not replace the dermal HA lost with age.

EFFECTIVE CONCENTRATION: 0.1-2%. Most serums are around 1-2%. Higher concentrations can feel sticky and give no extra benefit.

DEEPER SKIN TONES (Monk 6-10): no tone-specific risks. Dehydration often shows as an ashy, dull, or grey cast on deeper skin, and humectant hydration visibly restores luminosity. HA is non-irritating, so it carries no PIH risk.

PAIRS WELL WITH: almost everything. It is especially useful with occlusives/emollients (ceramides, squalane, dimethicone) that seal in the water it draws up, and it buffers drier actives such as retinol, salicylic acid, and vitamin C. Apply to damp skin.
CONFLICTS: no chemical conflicts. In very dry climates (low humidity) without an occlusive on top, humectants can pull water from deeper skin and make skin feel tighter.

SENSITIZATION / OVERUSE RISKS: very low; allergic reactions are rare. Problems usually come from other ingredients in the formula (fragrance, preservatives) rather than HA itself.
