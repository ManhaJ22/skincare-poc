Ingredient: Hyaluronic Acid
INCI name: Sodium Hyaluronate (salt form), Hyaluronic Acid
Also known as: HA, Hyaluronan, Sodium Hyaluronate

Mechanism of action:
Hyaluronic acid is a glycosaminoglycan naturally present in connective tissue, synovial fluid, and the extracellular matrix of skin. It is a powerful humectant — it holds up to 1,000x its weight in water by creating a hydrogen-bonded network that traps water molecules. Primary actions:
1. Draws moisture from environment into the outer skin layers (humectant)
2. High molecular weight forms a surface film → reduces TEWL (transepidermal water loss)
3. Low molecular weight penetrates the stratum corneum → delivers hydration to deeper layers
4. Plumps the skin acutely → reduces appearance of fine lines from dehydration
5. Supports wound healing through interaction with CD44 receptors

Molecular weight matters:
- High MW (>1 MDa): Surface humectant, film-forming, reduces TEWL. Best for normal-dry skin.
- Medium MW (50kDa-1MDa): Penetrates outer layers, provides structural support
- Low MW (<50 kDa): Penetrates more deeply, more anti-inflammatory but can cause irritation in some
- Hydrolyzed HA/Nano HA (<10 kDa): Deepest penetration — used in some clinical products
- Best formulas combine multiple molecular weights (multi-weight HA)

Application technique — critical:
Apply to DAMP skin (immediately post-cleansing or after misting). On completely dry skin, especially in low-humidity environments, HA draws from deeper skin layers → causes transient tightness. With moisture present on the surface → draws that water instead → plumping and comfortable.

Monk tone specific notes:
- ALL tones: Universal ingredient. Dehydration affects all Monk tones equally. No tone-specific concerns.
- Tones 7-10: Dehydration on deeper skin tones can exaggerate ashiness and reduce luminosity. Consistent HA use improves radiance and skin texture regardless of other treatments.
- Note: HA does NOT address hyperpigmentation. It is a hydration ingredient only.

Effective concentrations:
- 0.1-1%: Effective humectant range. Higher % is not necessarily better — what matters is molecular weight and formulation, not concentration.
- Most serums: 1-2% sodium hyaluronate (salt form is more stable in formula)

Interactions and combinations:
- Works with everything. Stable across pH ranges. No known negative interactions.
- Best combined with an occlusive (ceramide cream, petrolatum) to seal in the moisture
- Does NOT replace an occlusive moisturizer — HA alone on very dry skin may increase water loss without a sealant on top

Sensitization risk: Extremely low. HA is naturally present in skin. True HA allergy has not been reliably documented.
Pregnancy safety: Safe
Photosensitivity: None
Available in: Serums, essences, moisturizers, toners, sheet masks, eye creams

Key studies:
- Papakonstantinou et al. (2012): Comprehensive review of HA in skin hydration and wound healing
- Gallo et al. (2011): Low vs high molecular weight HA effects on skin penetration
