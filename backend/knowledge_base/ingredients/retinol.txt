INGREDIENT: Retinol (and related retinoids: retinal/retinaldehyde, retinyl palmitate, granactive retinoid/HPR, adapalene; prescription tretinoin)

WHAT IT DOES AT A SKIN LEVEL: retinol is converted in the skin to retinaldehyde and then retinoic acid, which binds nuclear retinoic acid receptors (RARs) and changes gene expression.
- It speeds keratinocyte turnover and normalizes shedding inside pores, which prevents comedones (clogs) and treats acne.
- It stimulates fibroblasts to make collagen and reduces collagen-degrading enzymes, which softens fine lines and improves texture.
- It disperses melanin and speeds the shedding of pigmented cells, which fades hyperpigmentation over months.

EFFECTIVE CONCENTRATION: beginners 0.1-0.3%; moderate 0.3-0.5%; advanced 0.5-1%. Retinal is about 10x more potent than retinol. Adapalene 0.1% is available OTC for acne. Start 2-3 nights a week and build up over 4-8 weeks. Results take 3-6 months.

DEEPER SKIN TONES (Monk 6-10): very effective for PIH and acne on deeper skin. However, "retinization" (redness, peeling, irritation) can trigger new PIH, so the low-and-slow approach is especially important. Buffer by applying after moisturizer (the "sandwich" method) and use daily sunscreen.

PAIRS WELL WITH: niacinamide (reduces irritation), hyaluronic acid, ceramides, peptides, sunscreen (required, because retinoids increase sun sensitivity).
CONFLICTS: do not use on the same night as AHA/BHA exfoliants (alternate nights instead), benzoyl peroxide (it can degrade some retinoids; adapalene is the exception), or high-strength L-ascorbic acid. Stop several days before waxing or facial procedures.

SENSITIZATION / OVERUSE RISKS: dryness, flaking, stinging, redness, and temporary "purging" breakouts in the first weeks. Overuse damages the barrier. Avoid during pregnancy or breastfeeding (retinoids are contraindicated). Not suitable for active eczema or rosacea flares without guidance from a dermatologist.
