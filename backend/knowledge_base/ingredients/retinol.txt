Ingredient: Retinol (Vitamin A)
INCI name: Retinol
Also known as: Vitamin A, retinoid (class name), pro-retinoic acid

The retinoid conversion pathway:
Retinol → Retinaldehyde → Retinoic acid (tretinoin — the active form)
Each conversion step reduces potency AND reduces irritation. Prescription tretinoin is strongest (most irritating). OTC retinol is gentler (requires two conversion steps). Other OTC derivatives (retinyl palmitate, retinyl acetate) are even weaker.

Mechanism of action (via retinoic acid):
1. Binds RAR (retinoic acid receptors) → upregulates cell turnover genes
2. Increases keratinocyte proliferation → accelerates skin renewal
3. Collagen synthesis stimulation → reduces photoaging, fine lines
4. Inhibits MMP-1 (collagenase) → prevents collagen breakdown
5. Normalizes follicular keratinization → comedolytic (clears clogged pores)
6. Inhibits melanin production → reduces hyperpigmentation
7. Increases epidermal thickness over time (initially causes thinning during retinization)

Concentration guide (OTC):
- 0.025-0.1%: Entry level — very gentle, for beginners or very sensitive skin
- 0.2%: Beginner-appropriate, meaningful efficacy with lower irritation
- 0.5%: Mid-strength — requires 4+ weeks of retinization at lower % first
- 1%: Strong — for experienced retinol users with fully adapted skin
- >1%: Prescription territory (tretinoin 0.025%, 0.05%, 0.1%)

Retinization period:
First 2-6 weeks: Skin may experience dryness, flaking, mild redness, temporary purging (breakouts of comedones pushed to surface). This is expected and temporary. Do NOT discontinue unless severe reaction occurs.

Monk tone specific notes — CRITICAL:
- Tones 1-4: Generally well-tolerated. Standard introduction protocol (1x/week → 2x/week → alternate nights → nightly over 8-12 weeks). Side effects are self-limiting.
- Tones 5-7: Introduce carefully. Retinization irritation CAN cause PIH. Use sandwich technique: moisturizer → 5 min wait → retinol → 5 min wait → moisturizer. Start 1x/week strictly. Pair with niacinamide on non-retinol nights for ongoing PI inflammation prevention.
- Tones 8-10: Maximum caution. The risk of PIH from retinol irritation is highest in the deepest Monk tones. Consider starting with The Ordinary Granactive Retinoid 2% (retinoid ester, converted without the conversion step causing irritation, less irritating than retinol) before switching to retinol. Sandwich technique mandatory. 1x/week for minimum 4 weeks before increasing.
- ALL tones: MANDATORY SPF 50 the morning after every retinol application. Retinol causes photosensitivity. UV exposure on retinized skin dramatically increases risk of sunburn and PIH.

Interactions:
- AHAs/BHAs: Do not use same evening. Alternating evenings is fine.
- Benzoyl peroxide: Oxidizes retinol → degrades actives. Do not layer together.
- Vitamin C: Some formulations are unstable together. Layer in AM (Vitamin C) and PM (retinol) instead.
- Niacinamide: Safe and synergistic — reduces irritation.

Absolute contraindications:
- Pregnancy (Category X — causes birth defects)
- Breastfeeding (caution, discuss with physician)
- Active eczema flares, rosacea flares, broken/sunburned skin

Sensitization risk: Moderate — retinization is a predictable temporary reaction, not sensitization
Pregnancy safety: CONTRAINDICATED — do not use during pregnancy or breastfeeding
Photosensitivity: Yes — mandatory AM SPF use throughout treatment

Alternatives for those who cannot use retinol:
- Azelaic acid (pregnancy-safe, gentler PIH treatment)
- Bakuchiol (plant retinol alternative — weaker but safe for pregnancy, study by Chaudhuri & Bojanowski 2014)
- Retinyl palmitate (very gentle, much weaker)

Key studies:
- Kligman & Willis (1975): Original tretinoin photoaging study
- Griffiths et al. (1995): Randomized trial — retinol vs retinoic acid efficacy comparison
- Mukherjee et al. (2006): Comprehensive retinoids review in photoaging
