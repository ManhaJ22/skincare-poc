INGREDIENT: Sunscreen filters / SPF (mineral: zinc oxide, titanium dioxide; chemical/organic: avobenzone, homosalate, octisalate, octocrylene, and others)

WHAT IT DOES AT A SKIN LEVEL: UV filters absorb (and to a small extent reflect/scatter) ultraviolet radiation before it reaches living skin cells. UVB causes burning and direct DNA damage. UVA penetrates deeper and drives photoaging, collagen breakdown, oxidative stress, and pigmentation. SPF measures UVB protection only; "broad spectrum" indicates UVA coverage. Zinc oxide covers UVB plus all of UVA. Titanium dioxide is strongest in UVB and short UVA. Iron oxides in tinted formulas block visible light, which also triggers pigmentation.

EFFECTIVE USE: SPF 30 minimum for daily use; SPF 50 for high exposure or for pigmentation concerns. Correct amount is about 1/4 teaspoon (two finger-lengths) for the face and neck. Reapply every 2 hours in direct sun and after swimming or sweating. Most people apply only 25-50% of the tested amount, which drastically lowers real-world protection.

DEEPER SKIN TONES (Monk 6-10):
- Melanin gives some intrinsic protection (roughly SPF 13 for very deep skin), but that is not enough to prevent hyperpigmentation, melasma, photoaging, or skin cancer. Daily SPF is still needed.
- Hyperpigmentation, PIH, and melasma are the most common concerns for deeper tones, and UV plus visible light make them worse. No brightening product works without daily sunscreen.
- White cast: mineral filters (especially titanium dioxide and non-micronized zinc) leave a white, grey, or purple cast on deeper skin. Options: chemical-filter sunscreens, tinted mineral sunscreens in a matching shade, or sheer gel/fluid formulas.

PAIRS WELL WITH: antioxidants (vitamin C, niacinamide), which mop up free radicals that get through sunscreen. Essential with any exfoliant or retinoid, which increase sun sensitivity.
CONFLICTS: apply sunscreen as the last skincare step in the morning; layering it under moisturizer dilutes it. Mixing it into foundation dilutes it as well.

SENSITIZATION / OVERUSE RISKS: chemical filters (especially oxybenzone and octocrylene) can sting or cause contact allergy in sensitive skin; mineral filters are the gentler option. Heavy formulas can clog pores in acne-prone skin, so choose non-comedogenic ones. There is no overuse risk.
