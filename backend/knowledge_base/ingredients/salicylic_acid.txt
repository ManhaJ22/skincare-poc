Ingredient: Salicylic Acid (BHA)
INCI name: Salicylic Acid
Also known as: BHA (beta-hydroxy acid), 2-hydroxybenzoic acid

What makes it a BHA:
The hydroxyl group is in the beta position (one carbon away from the carboxyl group). This makes salicylic acid oil-soluble — unlike AHAs (water-soluble). Oil-solubility is the key: salicylic acid can penetrate through the sebum that fills pores and dissolve the keratin plugs (dead skin + sebum) causing blackheads, whiteheads, and clogged pores. AHAs exfoliate the surface; BHA exfoliates inside the follicle.

Mechanism of action:
1. Oil-soluble penetration into follicles → dissolves sebum + keratin debris (comedolytic)
2. Desmolytic effect → breaks bonds between corneocytes → accelerates shedding of dead skin cells
3. Anti-inflammatory: Salicylic acid is structurally related to aspirin (acetylsalicylic acid) → inhibits prostaglandin synthesis → reduces skin inflammation
4. Mild antibacterial against C. acnes (acne-causing bacteria) at higher concentrations

Effective concentrations:
- 0.5%: Gentle exfoliation — appropriate for sensitive skin introduction, pregnancy-adjacent use discussions
- 1%: Mid-range — effective for mild congestion, blackheads
- 2% (OTC maximum): Full comedolytic effect — maximum allowed in OTC products by FDA
- >2%: Requires prescription (peels: 20-30%)

pH matters for activity:
- Active (non-ionized) form is below pKa of 2.97
- At pH 3.0-4.0: Full activity
- Rinse-off products (cleansers): pH less important — limited contact time reduces depth of penetration and irritation
- Leave-on products (toners, serums): pH critical — products at pH 3-4 are fully active

Product format matters — CRITICAL:
- Rinse-off (cleansers): Reduced contact time → gentler, less risk, appropriate for sensitive and deeper skin tones
- Leave-on (toners, serums, pads): Maximum efficacy AND maximum irritation risk — introduce slowly

Monk tone specific notes — CAUTION INGREDIENT:
- Tones 1-4: Well-tolerated at 2% leave-on. Standard introduction: 2-3x per week, build up over 4-6 weeks. Highly effective for oily/acne-prone skin.
- Tones 5-6: Introduce cautiously at leave-on 2%. Start 1-2x per week. Watch closely for signs of irritation (redness, burning). If any irritation, reduce frequency. Consider starting with 1% concentration.
- Tones 7-10: HIGH PIH RISK. Salicylic acid irritation triggers post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation in deeper skin tones at a higher rate than lighter tones. Risk management:
  (a) Start with rinse-off BHA (cleanser) only — provides benefit with lower irritation risk
  (b) If moving to leave-on: start at 0.5-1%, 1x per week maximum
  (c) Pair with niacinamide (PI inflammation management) on off-nights
  (d) If any darkening at application sites → discontinue and switch to azelaic acid
  (e) Mandatory SPF after any BHA use
  (f) Many dermatologists recommend azelaic acid as the primary exfoliant for tones 8-10 due to superior safety profile

Interactions:
- Other exfoliants (AHAs, retinol): Do not combine same evening — additive irritation
- Niacinamide: Safe combination — reduces BHA irritation
- SPF: Required after any BHA use due to increased UV sensitivity
- Aspirin allergy: Cross-reactivity is possible — patch test required

Contraindications:
- Pregnancy: FDA Category C at high concentrations/systemic use. Rinse-off OTC formulas at 2% are generally considered low risk — recommend consulting OB-GYN. Avoid leave-on at 2% during pregnancy.
- Aspirin (salicylate) allergy: Potential cross-reactivity
- Broken or irritated skin
- Eczema-prone or rosacea-prone skin

Sensitization risk: Moderate-high for leave-on at 2% in deeper skin tones
Pregnancy safety: Caution — consult OB-GYN. Avoid leave-on 2% during pregnancy.
Photosensitivity: Yes — wear SPF the morning after use
Available in: Cleansers, toners, serums, spot treatments, pads

Key studies:
- Grimes (1999): Salicylic acid peels for PIH in darker skin tones (clinical trial)
- Arif (2015): Salicylic acid review in acne treatment
- Kaminsky (1999): BHA mechanism and clinical applications
